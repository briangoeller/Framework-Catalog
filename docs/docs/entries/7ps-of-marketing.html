7Ps of Marketing

Also known as: Extended Marketing Mix

framework · marketing · organizing-schema

Booms and Bitner's extension adding people, process, and physical evidence for services.

The 7Ps of Marketing was articulated by Bernard Booms and Mary Jo Bitner in their 1981 paper 'Marketing Strategies and Organization Structures for Service Firms' as an extension of McCarthy's 4Ps to address the distinctive characteristics of services that the original framework underweighted. The four characteristics of services that motivate the extension are intangibility (services cannot be touched or stored), perishability (services consumed at the moment of production cannot be inventoried), inseparability (production and consumption typically simultaneous), and variability (services vary substantially across providers and even occasions). The three additional Ps address these characteristics: People — the human actors performing and receiving the service (employees, customers, other customers in the experience); Process — the procedures, mechanisms, and flow of activities by which the service is delivered; Physical Evidence — the tangible cues (environment, brochures, equipment, decor) that signal service quality before and during consumption. The 7Ps framework dominates services-marketing education and has been substantially adopted in industries including hospitality, healthcare, education, financial services, and professional services. The framework retains the 4Ps strengths and weaknesses while addressing services-specific concerns; it has been substantially complemented by Service-Dominant Logic (Vargo-Lusch 2004) and other contemporary services-marketing developments.

Core components

  • Original 4Ps: Product, Price, Place, Promotion
  • Three additional Ps for services: People (employees, customers)
  • Process (procedures and flow)
  • Physical Evidence (tangible cues)
  • Connection to four characteristics of services: intangibility, perishability, inseparability, variability
  • Distinction from goods-marketing emphasis
  • Foundation of services-marketing as distinct discipline
  • Subsequent complement by Service-Dominant Logic and service-design frameworks

Primary use case

Services-marketing analysis and planning; foundation for hospitality, healthcare, education, financial services, professional services marketing; reference framework in services-marketing education and certification; basis for service-blueprinting and service-design practice; integration with customer-experience management.

Common criticisms

  • Inherits 4Ps producer-centric framing concerns
  • substantial conceptual overlap among the three additional Ps (People-Process-Physical Evidence often interact in ways the framework doesn't fully resolve)
  • some services-marketing scholars argue the goods-services distinction is less fundamental than the 7Ps assumes — Service-Dominant Logic (Vargo-Lusch) reframes all marketing as service-marketing
  • framework less adopted in pure-product contexts
  • commercial certification industry has produced compliance-style 7Ps adoption
  • 'Physical Evidence' is awkward category that overlaps with environmental psychology in ways the framework doesn't fully integrate
  • like the 4Ps, descriptive rather than prescriptive — doesn't tell you what to do with each P
  • modern digital and platform services raise questions about how to operationalize Place and Physical Evidence.
