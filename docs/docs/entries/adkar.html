ADKAR

framework · management · organizing-schema

Awareness, Desire, Knowledge, Ability, Reinforcement — Prosci's individual-change model.

ADKAR is a sequential model of individual-level change developed by Jeff Hiatt of Prosci in the late 1990s and presented in his 2006 book ADKAR: A Model for Change in Business, Government and Our Community. Its premise is that organizational change succeeds only when each affected individual successfully traverses five sequential transitions: Awareness of the need for change; Desire to participate and support the change; Knowledge of how to change; Ability to implement required skills and behaviors; and Reinforcement to sustain the change. ADKAR pairs with Prosci's three-phase organizational change-management process (Prepare for change, Manage change, Sustain change) and is delivered through a substantial certification and consulting practice. The model is widely used in corporate change-management programs alongside or as a substitute for Kotter's framework.

Primary use case

Individual-level change management within larger organizational change initiatives; structured change-readiness assessment; complement to process-focused change models like Kotter's; foundation for Prosci's certification-based consulting practice.

Common criticisms

  • Proprietary and consulting-driven, with substantial barrier to academic validation
  • sequential structure assumes individuals progress in order, which empirical change research rarely supports
  • treats change linearly when reality involves multiple iterations and setbacks
  • certification industry creates professional incentives independent of methodological merit
  • less academic peer-reviewed validation than Kotter or Lewin
  • risks reducing change leadership to checklist-driven compliance.
