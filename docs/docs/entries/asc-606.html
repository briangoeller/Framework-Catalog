ASC 606

Also known as: Revenue from Contracts with Customers

framework · accounting · regulatory-standard

FASB Accounting Standards Codification topic establishing a five-step framework for revenue recognition; converged with IFRS 15.

ASC 606 (Revenue from Contracts with Customers) is the FASB Accounting Standards Codification topic establishing a single, principle-based revenue recognition model for nearly all contracts with customers across industries. Issued jointly with the IASB's IFRS 15 in May 2014 (ASU 2014-09), it superseded approximately 180 pieces of industry-specific revenue guidance under legacy US GAAP. The standard prescribes a five-step model: identify the contract, identify the performance obligations, determine the transaction price, allocate the price to performance obligations, and recognize revenue as obligations are satisfied. Effective dates were 2018 for public business entities and 2019 for others, after a one-year deferral. Implementation required substantial system changes, cross-functional process redesign, and ongoing judgment in areas such as variable consideration, contract modifications, principal-versus-agent analysis, and licenses of intellectual property.

Core components

  • Five-step model: 1) Identify the contract
  • 2) Identify performance obligations
  • 3) Determine transaction price
  • 4) Allocate transaction price to performance obligations
  • 5) Recognize revenue when (or as) performance obligations are satisfied
  • Variable consideration with constraint
  • Significant financing components
  • Principal-versus-agent considerations
  • Licenses of intellectual property (right-to-use vs right-to-access)
  • Contract costs (incremental costs of obtaining and fulfilling)
  • Disaggregation and contract balance disclosures

Primary use case

Revenue recognition by all US GAAP reporters across industries (with limited scope exclusions for leases, insurance, financial instruments, and certain guarantees and nonmonetary exchanges); basis for reported revenue and gross-margin trends in SEC filings; input to executive compensation tied to revenue, debt covenants, and M&A pricing.

Common criticisms

  • Implementation cost was substantial — Deloitte and PwC implementation surveys from 2017-2018 documented multi-million-dollar programs at large filers, raising cost-benefit questions for what was nominally a simplification project
  • subsequent ASUs (over a dozen targeted clarifications between 2015 and 2020) indicated the original guidance was insufficiently complete despite its principles-based framing
  • the variable-consideration constraint and significant-financing-component thresholds rely on management estimates susceptible to earnings-management pressure (academic studies including Tutticci and others have examined post-606 revenue smoothing patterns)
  • convergence with IFRS 15 has produced application differences in practice — notably around licenses of intellectual property, sales-based royalties, and shipping arrangements — that undermine the joint-project premise
  • software, telecom, and construction industries each required extensive industry-specific implementation guidance that the standard's general framework did not provide cleanly
  • smaller filers report disproportionate implementation burden.
