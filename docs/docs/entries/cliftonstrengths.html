CliftonStrengths

Also known as: StrengthsFinder

framework · psychology · organizing-schema

Gallup's 34 talent themes used for strengths-based development.

CliftonStrengths (originally StrengthsFinder) is a personality assessment developed by Donald O. Clifton at Selection Research Inc. (later acquired by Gallup), based on Clifton's decades of research interviewing high performers across roles and industries. The instrument identifies an individual's signature themes from a taxonomy of 34 'talent themes' organized into four leadership domains (Strategic Thinking, Relationship Building, Influencing, Executing). The framework's theoretical commitment, presented in Buckingham and Clifton's Now, Discover Your Strengths (2001) and Rath's StrengthsFinder 2.0 (2007), is that performance excellence comes from developing innate talents into strengths through deliberate practice, rather than from remediating weaknesses. CliftonStrengths has been taken by tens of millions of people, predominantly through corporate purchase. Independent psychometric evaluation is limited, and the 34-theme taxonomy is a Gallup proprietary structure rather than one derived from published factor-analytic methods, distinguishing it from established personality frameworks like the Big Five and HEXACO.

Primary sources

Buckingham, M. & Clifton, D.O. (2001). Now, Discover Your Strengths, Rath, T. (2007). StrengthsFinder 2.0, Gallup. Technical Report on the CliftonStrengths Assessment (multiple revisions) high

Band notes

Validation primarily from Gallup (proprietor); limited independent replication.

Core components

  • 34 talent themes across four domains: Strategic Thinking, Relationship Building, Influencing, Executing
  • Top 5 'signature themes' as default report
  • Full 34-theme report (paid upgrade)
  • Strengths-based development philosophy
  • Theme descriptions and action ideas
  • Connection to Gallup Q12 employee engagement instrument

Primary use case

Employee development and team-building in corporate contexts; basis for strengths-based coaching; manager development for individualizing direct-report engagement; college and student development programs; widely used HR and talent-management instrument.

Common criticisms

  • Validation primarily from Gallup with limited independent peer-reviewed replication
  • 34-theme taxonomy is proprietary rather than derived from standard factor-analytic methods
  • substantial overlap among themes raises questions about whether 34 distinct constructs are warranted
  • assessment is essentially developmental — not designed for selection — but is sometimes used for selection inappropriately
  • 'strengths-based' philosophy is intuitive but the empirical evidence that focusing on strengths outperforms balanced development is mixed
  • commercial structure incentivizes adoption regardless of evidence
  • signature-themes-only default may give misleading partial picture of someone's full talent profile.
