Eightfold Path

framework · theology · contemplative-tradition

Buddhist path: right view, intention, speech, action, livelihood, effort, mindfulness, concentration.

The Eightfold Path is the fourth of Buddhism's Four Noble Truths and articulates the Buddhist way of cessation of suffering through eight integrated aspects of practice. Traditionally grouped into three trainings — wisdom (right view, right intention), ethical conduct (right speech, right action, right livelihood), and mental discipline (right effort, right mindfulness, right concentration) — the path is understood as simultaneous rather than sequential cultivation. The framework is foundational across Buddhist traditions though specific interpretations vary substantially between Theravada, Mahayana, and Vajrayana lineages.

Primary use case

Foundational Buddhist practice across all major traditions; ethical and contemplative framework adapted in secular mindfulness contexts.

Common criticisms

  • Translation of 'right' (samma) is culturally loaded
  • secularized interpretations strip the framework from its Four Noble Truths context
  • tension between simultaneous cultivation and pedagogical sequencing
  • differences between traditional interpretations make 'the' Eightfold Path somewhat misleading.
