Ethnography

framework · anthropology · structured-empirical

Immersive long-term fieldwork yielding detailed accounts of cultural practices.

Ethnography is the foundational research methodology of cultural anthropology — immersive long-term fieldwork (typically 12+ months) producing detailed written accounts of cultural practices, beliefs, and social life through participant observation, interviews, and substantial cultural immersion. The methodology was substantially established by Bronisław Malinowski through his Trobriand Islands fieldwork (1914-1918, with Argonauts of the Western Pacific 1922 substantially codifying the methodology) and Franz Boas's substantial American anthropology fieldwork tradition. Malinowski's foundational methodological commitments include: living among the studied community for extended periods; learning the local language; documenting daily life and seasonal cycles; substantial data collection through field notes, interviews, photographs, genealogies, kinship diagrams; producing 'monographs' synthesizing fieldwork findings into coherent ethnographic accounts. Subsequent development includes substantial methodological refinement (Bernard's Research Methods in Anthropology providing substantial systematization), digital ethnography (online and virtual community study), multi-sited ethnography (George Marcus's substantial 1995 articulation for studying transnational connections), institutional ethnography (Dorothy Smith's feminist methodology), and substantial extension beyond anthropology into sociology, organizational studies, design research, healthcare, education, and other fields. The 1980s 'reflexive turn' substantially complicated ethnographic methodology — Writing Culture (Clifford-Marcus 1986), Marcus and Fischer's Anthropology as Cultural Critique (1986), and broader 'crisis of representation' literature substantially documented concerns about ethnographic authority, the politics of representation, the substantial gap between fieldwork experience and ethnographic text, and the substantial challenge of representing 'other' cultures from positions of substantial epistemic and political asymmetry. Contemporary ethnographic practice substantially incorporates reflexive awareness of these concerns while continuing to produce substantial ethnographic research. Malinowski's posthumously published 1967 A Diary in the Strict Sense of the Term substantially complicated the heroic-fieldworker image — showing Malinowski's substantial frustration, racism, and personal complications during fieldwork that the official methodology obscured.

Core components

  • Long-term immersive fieldwork (typically 12+ months)
  • Participant observation as principal technique
  • Substantial language learning
  • Field notes, interviews, genealogies, kinship diagrams as data
  • Monographic synthesis as principal output
  • Foundation in Malinowski's Trobriand Islands work
  • Substantial Boasian American tradition
  • 1980s reflexive turn complicating methodology
  • Recent extensions: digital ethnography, multi-sited ethnography, institutional ethnography
  • Substantial extension beyond anthropology into sociology, design research, healthcare, education
  • Foundation for cross-cultural understanding

Primary use case

Foundational research methodology in cultural anthropology globally; basis for substantial work across all anthropological theoretical traditions; reference methodology in anthropology education globally; foundation for substantial cross-cultural understanding and area-studies research; integration with broader qualitative-research traditions; pedagogical foundation in anthropology curricula; substantial extension to sociology (urban ethnography), design research (UX research, service design ethnography), healthcare research, education research, organizational studies; foundation for substantial commercial ethnographic research industry particularly in design and consumer research.

Common criticisms

  • Substantial 1980s reflexive critique: Writing Culture (Clifford-Marcus 1986) and broader 'crisis of representation' literature substantially documented concerns about: ethnographic authority (whose voice is privileged in ethnographic accounts)
  • politics of representation (how does the anthropologist represent the studied community, and with whose authorization?)
  • substantial gap between fieldwork experience and ethnographic text
  • epistemic and political asymmetries in cross-cultural representation
  • substantial postcolonial concerns that ethnography emerged from colonial-era anthropology with substantial complications that contemporary ethnography hasn't fully resolved
  • commercial 'ethnographic research' in design and consumer research often involves substantially shorter fieldwork (days or weeks rather than 12+ months) than anthropological ethnography while invoking the methodology's substantial intellectual prestige — ethnography-as-buzzword problem
  • integration with quantitative methods is genuinely difficult — ethnographic findings are difficult to integrate with statistical analysis
  • ethical complications including informed consent across cultural contexts, payment to research participants, risk of harm through publication
  • cross-cultural variation in research ethics frameworks creates additional complications
  • Malinowski's posthumously published 1967 Diary substantially complicated the heroic-fieldworker image — showing personal complications and attitudes that official methodology obscured
  • substantial extension beyond anthropology has produced 'ethnography' that varies substantially in fidelity to anthropological methodology
  • tendency for ethnographic findings to be difficult to generalize beyond specific studied contexts.
