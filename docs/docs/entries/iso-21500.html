ISO 21500

framework · project management · regulatory-standard

International standard providing guidance on project management concepts and processes, foundational to the ISO 21500 series.

ISO 21500:2012 (Guidance on project management) was the first International Organization for Standardization standard providing high-level guidance on project management concepts, processes, and terminology applicable to any type of organization. Developed by ISO/TC 258 (Project, programme and portfolio management), it represented a deliberate synthesis of PMBOK, PRINCE2, IPMA, and other national standards rather than competition with them, with the goal of providing a consensus international vocabulary and framework. The 2012 edition described 39 processes across five process groups and ten subject groups paralleling but not replicating PMBOK structure. ISO 21500:2012 was withdrawn in 2021 and replaced by ISO 21502:2020 (project management practices) plus a new ISO 21500:2021 covering project, programme, and portfolio management context and concepts. The 2012 edition retains historical importance as the first international project-management standard.

Year / Decade

Primary sources

Core components

  • Five process groups (initiating, planning, implementing, controlling, closing)
  • Ten subject groups (integration, stakeholder, scope, resource, time, cost, risk, quality, procurement, communication)
  • 39 processes mapped across the process group / subject group matrix
  • Project lifecycle and project environment context
  • Concepts and terminology section providing consensus vocabulary
  • Designed for non-prescriptive guidance use rather than as a methodology

Primary use case

International reference standard for project management vocabulary and high-level process structure (2012-2021 period); input to organizational and national project management framework development; translation reference between PMBOK, PRINCE2, IPMA, and other national frameworks; academic and policy reference in international project management research; foundation for ISO 21502 and the broader 21500-series successor standards.

Common criticisms

  • ISO 21500:2012 was criticized as a least-common-denominator synthesis that added little above the more-developed national standards it borrowed from — Crawford, Pollack, and others noted the 2012 standard lacked the operational specificity of PMBOK or PRINCE2 while not introducing substantive new guidance
  • voluntary-standard status with no certification scheme and no regulatory adoption produced low practical uptake
  • the 39-process structure tracked PMBOK closely enough to invite 'why not just use PMBOK' critiques
  • the standard was developed under PMI-heavy committee influence, with European and Asian participants noting a North American methodological bias
  • the 2021 withdrawal and replacement by ISO 21502 reflects ISO's own assessment that the 2012 framework needed substantial revision
  • the standard's lifetime produced limited empirical research base, leaving its effectiveness essentially unevaluated independently of derivative national frameworks
  • small-organization applicability was poor.
