Kotter's 8-Step Change Model

framework · management · organizing-schema

Sequential change leadership steps from urgency through anchoring in culture.

John Kotter's 8-Step Change Model, presented in his 1995 Harvard Business Review article 'Leading Change: Why Transformation Efforts Fail' and the 1996 book Leading Change, prescribes a sequential leadership process for major organizational change: 1) Establish a sense of urgency, 2) Create a guiding coalition, 3) Develop a vision and strategy, 4) Communicate the change vision, 5) Empower employees for broad-based action, 6) Generate short-term wins, 7) Consolidate gains and produce more change, 8) Anchor new approaches in the culture. The framework is grounded in Kotter's observational studies of large-scale change efforts and identifies common failure modes at each step. In Accelerate (2014), Kotter substantially revised the model toward eight 'accelerators' operating in a continuous network alongside the traditional hierarchy, addressing critiques about the original sequential framing's poor fit for fast-moving environments.

Core components

  • Eight sequential steps: Urgency
  • Guiding Coalition
  • Vision and Strategy
  • Communicate Vision
  • Empower Action
  • Short-term Wins
  • Consolidate Gains
  • Anchor in Culture
  • Failure-mode mapping at each step
  • 2014 Accelerate revision with dual operating system (hierarchy plus network)

Primary use case

Large-scale organizational change leadership; structuring change-management initiatives; communicating change vision; teaching tool in MBA leadership courses.

Common criticisms

  • Based on observational case work rather than rigorous empirical testing
  • sequential structure poorly fits continuous and concurrent change in modern environments (Kotter himself addressed this in 2014)
  • doesn't specify when each step is 'complete' or how to handle setbacks
  • oversimplifies political and power dynamics
  • assumes top-down change leadership and underweights bottom-up emergent change
  • the 70% failure-rate claim Kotter is famous for has been challenged as not based on systematic data
  • framework can become a checklist that misses context-specific challenges.
