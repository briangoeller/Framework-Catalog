Practice Theory

framework · anthropology · organizing-schema

Bourdieu, Ortner, and Sahlins on how practices reproduce and transform social structure.

Practice Theory is the family of theoretical frameworks emerging in anthropology and sociology in the 1970s-80s focused on how everyday practices simultaneously reproduce and transform social structure — providing alternative to both structuralist frameworks (which underweight agency) and purely actor-centered frameworks (which underweight structural conditions). Foundational figures include Pierre Bourdieu (substantial articulation through Outline of a Theory of Practice 1972, The Logic of Practice 1980, Distinction 1979 — though Bourdieu is sociologist with substantial anthropological influence), Marshall Sahlins (Historical Metaphors and Mythical Realities 1981, Islands of History 1985 substantial historical-anthropology application), and Sherry Ortner (substantial articulation in 'Theory in Anthropology Since the Sixties' 1984 Comparative Studies in Society and History, with substantial subsequent development). Practice theory's central commitments include: (1) social structure exists through and is reproduced by practices — habitual ways of doing, perceiving, valuing that participants internalize through socialization; (2) Bourdieu's 'habitus' — durable dispositions that participants acquire from social position and that produce practice without requiring conscious calculation; (3) practices reproduce structure but also transform it through accumulated improvisations, conflicts, and adaptations; (4) attention to power, capital (economic, cultural, social, symbolic), and field-specific dynamics; (5) integration of structuralist and Marxist insights with attention to lived practice. Sahlins's substantial historical anthropology applied practice theory to Pacific colonial encounters, particularly Captain Cook's Hawaiian death (substantial debate with Gananath Obeyesekere about cross-cultural understanding and structural reproduction in colonial contexts). The framework substantially shaped contemporary anthropology by providing analytical apparatus that bridges structure-agency tension. Practice Theory has substantial influence beyond anthropology — substantial sociology adoption, organizational studies (Schatzki, Reckwitz), education research, healthcare research. Critics argue Bourdieu's habitus is substantially deterministic despite practice-theory rhetoric of agency; that practice theory's analytical apparatus is genuinely demanding without proportional empirical payoff; and that the framework risks reifying 'practices' as analytical units in ways that don't substantively reflect how participants experience their actions.

Primary use case

Major framework in contemporary anthropology and sociology; basis for substantial work on social reproduction, inequality, cultural reproduction, organizational dynamics; reference framework in anthropology education globally; foundation for substantial empirical work on class, education, taste, and other social-reproduction phenomena; integration with broader social theory; pedagogical foundation in social-theoretical curricula; influence on organizational studies, education research, healthcare research, business consulting; foundation for substantial commercial applications particularly in workplace culture and organizational change.

Common criticisms

  • Bourdieu's habitus has been substantially criticized as deterministic despite practice-theory rhetoric of agency — durable dispositions acquired from social position can produce reproduction without substantive transformation, leaving the framework's promise of explaining change unfulfilled
  • the analytical apparatus is genuinely demanding (habitus, field, capital types, doxa, hexis) without proportional empirical payoff in many applications
  • commercial 'practice' analysis in business contexts often invokes Bourdieusian terminology decoratively without substantive engagement with the framework
  • cross-cultural application of European-developed framework has uneven results
  • Bourdieu's substantial French intellectual context shapes specific commitments that don't always travel
  • integration with substantively different practice-theoretical variants (Sahlins, Ortner, Schatzki, Reckwitz) creates which-version-when ambiguity
  • the framework's mediating position between structure and agency means it can be invoked to support substantively different analytical conclusions
  • tendency to retrospectively explain outcomes through habitus and capital without specifying predictive constraints
  • integration with substantively quantitative empirical methods has been productive but contested
  • the framework's substantial influence on educational sociology has produced some substantively reductive analyses of educational inequality that more recent empirical work has complicated
  • Sahlins-Obeyesekere debate about Captain Cook represents substantial intra-discipline disagreement about how practice theory applies to colonial contact
  • recent anthropology has substantially returned to ethnographic particularity rather than practice-theoretical generalization in some sectors.
