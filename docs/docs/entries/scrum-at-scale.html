Scrum at Scale

Also known as: S@S

framework · computer science · organizing-schema

Sutherland's framework for scaling Scrum across an organization through Scrum of Scrums and Executive Action Teams.

Scrum at Scale (S@S) is the Scrum-scaling framework developed by Jeff Sutherland and Scrum Inc. and released in 2014 onward, articulated through The Scrum at Scale Guide (2018, with subsequent revisions). The framework extends Scrum to large-scale organizations through modular composition: a Scrum of Scrums (SoS) coordinates Scrum Teams; a Scrum of Scrum of Scrums (SoSoS) coordinates SoS units; a Meta-Scrum coordinates Product Owners across teams. Two cycles run in parallel: the Scrum Master Cycle (focused on how work is delivered, addressing process and team-coordination) and the Product Owner Cycle (focused on what is delivered, addressing product strategy and prioritization). Scrum at Scale is positioned by Sutherland as more modular and scalable than competing frameworks, with the framework adaptable from small to enterprise scale. Scrum Inc. provides training and certification infrastructure including Scrum at Scale Practitioner and Trainer certifications. The framework competes with SAFe, LeSS, Nexus, and Disciplined Agile in the agile-scaling-framework landscape.

Core components

  • Modular scaling structure: Scrum Team (5-9 people, standard Scrum)
  • Scrum of Scrums (SoS): coordinates 4-5 Scrum Teams with SoS Master leading
  • Scrum of Scrum of Scrums (SoSoS): coordinates multiple SoS units
  • Executive Action Team (EAT): top-level coordination team
  • Two parallel cycles: Scrum Master Cycle — focused on how work is delivered, addressing process improvement, team coordination, impediment removal, with components Continuous Improvement and Impediment Removal, Cross-Team Coordination, Deployment
  • Product Owner Cycle — focused on what is delivered, addressing product strategy and prioritization, with components Strategic Vision, Backlog Prioritization, Backlog Decomposition and Refinement, Release Planning
  • Meta-Scrum: coordinating Product Owners across teams, led by Chief Product Owner
  • Executive MetaScrum (EMS): top-level Meta-Scrum with executive participation
  • Modular adoption: organizations adopt components as needed rather than full framework
  • Common framework events: cross-team standups, cross-team review, cross-team retrospective
  • Distinction from Nexus: Scrum at Scale is more modular and supports broader scaling beyond 3-9 teams
  • Nexus has fixed structure
  • Distinction from SAFe: Scrum at Scale is substantially lighter than SAFe
  • SAFe provides more comprehensive enterprise structure
  • Distinction from LeSS: both are lightweight Scrum-extension approaches
  • LeSS is Vodde-Larman independent framework while Scrum at Scale is Sutherland-Scrum-Inc affiliated
  • Scrum Inc. training and certification: Scrum@Scale Practitioner and Trainer credentials
  • Application contexts: enterprise Scrum scaling, particularly organizations seeking modular adoption rather than comprehensive prescription

Primary use case

Scrum-scaling framework for enterprise agile adoption with modular scaling beyond single-product team structure; applied principally in: enterprise Scrum scaling where modular composition supports incremental adoption; large-scale agile transformation programs; Scrum Inc. training and certification ecosystem: Scrum@Scale Practitioner, Trainer, Coach certifications; academic and professional reference in agile scaling and software-engineering methodology literature; Sutherland's foundational role provides intellectual continuity with Scrum tradition (Sutherland being Scrum co-creator alongside Schwaber); complementary to (and competitive with) SAFe, LeSS, Nexus, Disciplined Agile, Spotify Model in the agile-scaling-framework landscape; particular adoption in 3M, Toyota Connected, and other enterprise implementations cited in Sutherland's writing.

Common criticisms

  • Scrum at Scale has had moderate adoption compared to SAFe (the dominant scaling framework by adoption metrics) and LeSS — practitioner debate documents that Scrum at Scale's modularity, while intellectually appealing, may underprovide guidance for organizations seeking comprehensive scaling structure that SAFe provides
  • the Scrum-Master-Cycle and Product-Owner-Cycle separation has been argued by some practitioners to introduce coordination complexity at scale that produces parallel-process overhead, with documented cases of cycles operating in tension rather than complement
  • the emphasis on Scrum-of-Scrums coordination structure has been argued to reproduce hierarchical coordination antipatterns that flat-team structures aim to avoid, with substantial debate about whether SoS / SoSoS structures genuinely improve coordination versus produce additional bureaucracy
  • the framework's modular adoption flexibility produces in-practice implementation variation that complicates comparative evaluation — different organizations adopting different Scrum at Scale components may produce substantially different operational realities under the same framework brand
  • competition with Nexus (both Scrum-extension frameworks from Scrum co-creators) has produced ongoing practitioner debate about whether the two frameworks duplicate scope or differentiate meaningfully — Sutherland (Scrum at Scale) and Schwaber (Nexus) developed their frameworks independently after departing the joint Scrum Alliance
  • empirical evidence on Scrum at Scale effectiveness versus alternative scaling frameworks is sparse, with most agile-scaling comparison research focusing on SAFe
  • the Scrum Inc. training and certification commercial infrastructure raises ordinary concerns about institutional incentives shaping framework promotion
  • integration with modern DevOps practices, platform engineering, and microservices architectures has been incompletely worked out in canonical Scrum at Scale literature
  • the Executive Action Team and Executive MetaScrum constructs have been argued by some practitioners to potentially reproduce executive-control antipatterns that agile principles aim to constrain.
