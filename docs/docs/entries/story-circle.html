Story Circle

framework · narrative studies · organizing-schema

Harmon's eight-step distillation of Campbell's monomyth for episodic storytelling.

The Story Circle is the eight-step narrative framework articulated by television writer and showrunner Dan Harmon (creator of Community, co-creator of Rick and Morty) as practical distillation of Joseph Campbell's monomyth (Hero with a Thousand Faces 1949) for episodic television storytelling. Harmon's eight steps form a circular structure: (1) A character is in a zone of comfort; (2) But they want something; (3) They enter an unfamiliar situation; (4) Adapt to it; (5) Get what they wanted; (6) Pay a heavy price for it; (7) Then return to their familiar situation; (8) Having changed. The framework's circular structure emphasizes that the protagonist returns to a similar situation but with substantial transformation — a 'descent and return' pattern that descends from Campbell's monomyth (departure, initiation, return) but is operationalized for the substantial constraints of episodic television (where each episode typically returns characters to the situation that enables the series to continue). Harmon developed the framework substantially through his work on television (substantial Channel 101 short-film community in early 2000s, then his television production), with the framework substantially circulated through online writing about his methodology and through interviews. The Story Circle has been substantially adopted by writers in television, comics, novels, and games — providing accessible practical framework for narrative development that's substantially less rigid than Campbell's elaborate seventeen-stage monomyth or Vogler's Writer's Journey adaptation (separately enriched). The framework's commercial success has been substantial in the streaming-television era, with substantial influence on showrunner methodology. Critics note the framework's substantial debt to Campbell brings forward Campbell's substantial issues — substantially Western androcentric framing, substantial overgeneralization of 'universal' narrative pattern, substantial assumptions about character transformation that don't apply to all stories. Some narrative theorists argue the framework's universality claims overstate what episodic television demonstrates.

Common criticisms

  • The framework's substantial debt to Campbell's monomyth brings forward Campbell's substantial issues — substantially Western androcentric framing (Maureen Murdock's 1990 Heroine's Journey provided substantial critique of Campbell's gendered framing), substantial overgeneralization of 'universal' narrative pattern, substantial assumptions about character transformation that don't apply to all stories
  • the framework's circular structure works well for episodic television but may not transfer cleanly to other forms
  • commercial popularization has produced compliance-style application — writers hitting the eight steps without substantive narrative quality
  • cross-cultural application of Campbell-derived framings has substantial limitations — many narrative traditions have substantially different structural conventions that the eight-step circle doesn't capture
  • tendency for Story Circle to be applied as checklist rather than as analytical framework
  • integration with character interiority, theme, atmosphere, and other dramatic dimensions is often weak
  • the framework works better for plot-driven episodic narratives than for character-driven, ensemble, or atmosphere-driven works
  • Harmon's substantial commercial success may have produced uncritical adoption of his methodology
  • cross-genre application varies — works better for adventure, action, and growth narratives than for tragedy, satire, or experimental work
  • some narrative theorists argue the framework substantially overstates universality of descent-and-return narrative pattern.
