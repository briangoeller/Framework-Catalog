TCFD

Also known as: Task Force on Climate-related Financial Disclosures

framework · governance and compliance · regulatory-standard

Voluntary framework for climate-related financial risk disclosures across governance, strategy, risk, and metrics.

The TCFD was established by the Financial Stability Board in 2015 under the chairmanship of Michael Bloomberg to develop voluntary, consistent climate-related financial risk disclosures for companies, banks, insurers, and asset owners. Its 2017 final recommendations organized disclosures around four thematic pillars — Governance, Strategy, Risk Management, and Metrics & Targets — with eleven recommended disclosures and supplemental guidance for the financial sector and four non-financial sectors. The framework distinguishes between physical risks (acute and chronic climate impacts) and transition risks (policy, technology, market, and reputational shifts associated with decarbonization), and recommends scenario analysis including a 2°C-or-lower scenario. The TCFD was disbanded in 2023 with monitoring of disclosures transferred to the IFRS Foundation, whose ISSB has since published IFRS S1 and S2 incorporating and extending the TCFD recommendations as a mandatory baseline.

Core components

  • Four pillars: Governance
  • Strategy
  • Risk Management
  • Metrics & Targets
  • Eleven recommended disclosures
  • Distinction between physical and transition risk
  • Scenario analysis (including 2°C-or-lower)
  • Sector-specific supplemental guidance for financial and four non-financial sectors

Primary use case

Voluntary climate-related financial disclosures incorporated into supervisory expectations (Bank of England PRA, NGFS, ECB), national mandatory regimes (UK 2022 mandatory TCFD-aligned disclosure), and the ISSB's IFRS S1/S2 successor standards.

Common criticisms

  • Voluntary nature and qualitative framing produced uneven adoption and disclosure quality
  • scenario analysis methodology under-specified, creating comparability problems
  • metric specifications thin in early versions
  • superseded by IFRS S2 from ISSB which formalizes much of the framework with greater specificity
  • integration with Scope 3 emissions disclosure remained contested.
