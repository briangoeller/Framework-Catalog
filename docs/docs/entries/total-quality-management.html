Total Quality Management

Also known as: TQM

framework · management · organizing-schema

Organization-wide focus on continuous quality improvement with customer at the center.

Total Quality Management is the organization-wide management philosophy that emerged from the post-WWII quality movement, integrating contributions from Walter A. Shewhart (statistical process control and the PDCA cycle at Bell Labs in the 1920s-30s), W. Edwards Deming (the System of Profound Knowledge, 14 Points for Management, and the Seven Deadly Diseases, including transformative work in postwar Japan from 1950 onward), Joseph M. Juran (the Quality Trilogy of planning, control, and improvement; the Pareto principle in quality), Philip B. Crosby (Quality is Free, zero defects, four absolutes), Kaoru Ishikawa (company-wide quality control, fishbone diagram, quality circles), and Armand Feigenbaum (who coined 'total quality control' in his 1951 book). TQM treats quality as the responsibility of everyone, customer satisfaction as the central objective, continuous improvement (kaizen) as the operating discipline, and statistical methods as the empirical foundation. The framework is the direct progenitor of ISO 9000, the Malcolm Baldrige Award (1987), the Deming Prize, and substantial later developments including Toyota Production System, Six Sigma, and Lean — though Deming himself was deeply critical of the management-by-objectives mindset that some TQM implementations carried forward.

Core components

  • PDCA cycle (Plan-Do-Check-Act, Shewhart-Deming)
  • Deming's 14 Points for Management
  • Deming's System of Profound Knowledge
  • Juran Quality Trilogy (planning, control, improvement)
  • Crosby's Four Absolutes and Zero Defects
  • Ishikawa's seven basic tools
  • Customer-centricity
  • Continuous improvement
  • Statistical process control
  • Quality circles
  • Cross-functional teams
  • Supplier partnerships

Primary use case

Foundation of the modern quality movement; basis for TPS, Six Sigma, Lean, ISO 9000, Malcolm Baldrige Award, Deming Prize; transformation of postwar Japanese manufacturing; corporate-wide quality programs; ongoing reference framework in operations management.

Common criticisms

  • TQM as labeled corporate program saw uneven Western implementation in the 1980s-90s, often missing the deeper philosophy while adopting tools
  • Deming was sharply critical of MBO and many of the practices that some TQM programs incorporated
  • quality-program initiatives often produced documentation overhead without lasting culture change
  • cultural transferability from Japanese context to Western firms remains contested
  • substantial overlap and rivalry with Six Sigma created which-program-when confusion
  • programs tied to consultants and certification rather than internal capability
  • criticized as having atrophied as a label even as its substantive practices live on in Lean and Six Sigma.
