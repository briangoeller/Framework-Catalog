Trompenaars' Cultural Dimensions

Also known as: Seven Dimensions of Culture

framework · management · organizing-schema

Alternative cross-cultural framework emphasizing relationships, time, and environment.

Fons Trompenaars and Charles Hampden-Turner's seven-dimension cross-cultural framework, presented in Riding the Waves of Culture (1997), offers an alternative to Hofstede's framework grounded in survey data from 30,000+ managers across 50+ countries. The dimensions are organized around three categories: relationships with people (Universalism vs Particularism, Individualism vs Communitarianism, Neutral vs Emotional, Specific vs Diffuse, Achievement vs Ascription); attitudes to time (Sequential vs Synchronic, Past-Present-Future orientation); and attitudes to environment (Internal vs External Control). The framework draws on Talcott Parsons' pattern variables and Florence Kluckhohn's value orientations as theoretical antecedents and is widely used in cross-cultural consulting and training, particularly within the THT Consulting practice that grew from the original work.

Originators

Primary sources

Trompenaars, F. & Hampden-Turner, C. (1997). Riding the Waves of Culture: Understanding Cultural Diversity in Business (2nd ed.) high

Core components

  • Universalism vs Particularism
  • Individualism vs Communitarianism
  • Neutral vs Emotional
  • Specific vs Diffuse
  • Achievement vs Ascription
  • Sequential vs Synchronic time
  • Internal vs External control of environment

Primary use case

Cross-cultural management training and consulting; complement or alternative to Hofstede in international management programs; cross-cultural negotiation preparation; foundation for THT's consulting practice.

Common criticisms

  • Same general critiques as Hofstede regarding national-cultural homogeneity, cultural change, and ecological fallacy
  • less academic peer-reviewed validation than Hofstede
  • dimensional independence questioned (some dimensions correlate substantially)
  • commercial nature of THT Consulting limits independent replication
  • survey methodology and item construction not as transparently documented as Hofstede's
  • dimensions sometimes accused of being culture-laden in their construction (e.g., privileging Western analytic categories).
