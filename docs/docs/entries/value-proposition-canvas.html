Value Proposition Canvas

Also known as: VPC

framework · management · organizing-schema

Aligns customer pains, gains, and jobs to product features and benefits.

The Value Proposition Canvas, introduced by Alexander Osterwalder, Yves Pigneur, Greg Bernarda, and Alan Smith in Value Proposition Design (2014), is a focused complement to the Business Model Canvas that zooms into two of its nine blocks — Value Propositions and Customer Segments — to provide more detailed structure for designing and validating product-market fit. The Customer Profile side decomposes the customer's situation into Jobs (functional, social, emotional), Pains (frustrations, obstacles, risks), and Gains (desired outcomes, benefits); the Value Map side decomposes the offering into Products & Services, Pain Relievers, and Gain Creators. 'Fit' between the two sides — particularly that pain relievers address ranked pains and gain creators address ranked gains — is the test of a strong value proposition.

Primary sources

Core components

  • Customer Profile (Jobs, Pains, Gains)
  • Value Map (Products & Services, Pain Relievers, Gain Creators)
  • Fit between the two sides
  • Ranking of pains and gains by importance
  • Integration with Business Model Canvas

Primary use case

Value proposition design and product-market fit analysis; structuring customer interviews and discovery research; complementing the Business Model Canvas with deeper customer-segment analysis.

Common criticisms

  • Like BMC, descriptive rather than predictive — provides structure for thinking without specifying analytical method
  • jobs-pains-gains can be filled in plausibly for almost any product without driving real differentiation
  • overlap with Christensen-Ulwick Jobs-to-Be-Done frameworks creates conceptual confusion
  • can become a workshop deliverable disconnected from genuine customer research
  • ranking pains and gains is methodologically thin without quantitative customer data.
