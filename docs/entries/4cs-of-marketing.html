4Cs of Marketing

framework · marketing · organizing-schema

Lauterborn's customer-centric reframe: consumer, cost, convenience, communication.

The 4Cs of Marketing was articulated by Robert F. Lauterborn in his 1990 Advertising Age article 'New Marketing Litany: Four Ps Passé; C-Words Take Over' as a customer-centric reframing of McCarthy's producer-centric 4Ps. Each C corresponds to a P viewed from the customer's perspective: Customer (or Consumer wants and needs) replaces Product — what does the customer actually want, not what does the producer want to sell; Cost (to the customer) replaces Price — total cost including time, effort, and switching costs, not just monetary price; Convenience replaces Place — how easily can the customer access the offering, not where does the producer want to distribute; Communication replaces Promotion — two-way dialogue rather than one-way producer-to-customer broadcasts. The framework reflects broader 1980s-1990s marketing developments emphasizing customer orientation, relationship marketing (Berry, Levitt), and dialogue-based marketing communication. The 4Cs gained substantial adoption particularly in marketing-strategy contexts emphasizing customer experience and digital marketing where two-way communication became central. The framework is typically presented as complement rather than replacement to the 4Ps — the same marketing-mix decisions analyzed from two perspectives. More recent extensions including the 7Cs (adding Credibility, Connection, Conversation in some formulations) have elaborated the customer-centric framing further.

Core components

  • Four customer-centric elements: Customer (replaces Product), Cost (replaces Price), Convenience (replaces Place), Communication (replaces Promotion)
  • Reframing of 4Ps from customer perspective
  • Connection to customer orientation and relationship marketing
  • Two-way Communication as alternative to one-way Promotion
  • Total Cost concept (beyond monetary price)
  • Subsequent extensions (7Cs and others)
  • Typically complement rather than replacement to 4Ps

Primary use case

Customer-centric marketing analysis particularly in service and digital contexts; complement to 4Ps in marketing strategy; foundation for customer-experience design and journey mapping; reference framework in customer-relationship-management literature; basis for some marketing-audit frameworks; integration with contemporary digital marketing where two-way communication is central.

Common criticisms

  • Translation of 4Ps to 4Cs is sometimes mechanical without substantive customer insight — relabeling Promotion as Communication doesn't automatically produce two-way dialogue
  • the reframing addresses 4Ps' producer-centric language but doesn't fundamentally change the categorical structure
  • multiple competing customer-centric reframings (4Cs has at least three distinct versions including Lauterborn's, Schultz et al.'s integrated marketing communications framing, and the Communication framework variant)
  • commercial adoption sometimes treats 4Cs as alternative to 4Ps rather than complement, producing confusion
  • like 4Ps, descriptive rather than prescriptive about which customer needs to address
  • framework's effectiveness depends substantially on actual customer research that goes beyond renaming categories
  • 'Cost' as total cost is conceptually rich but operationally challenging.
