AIDAS ⚑

framework · marketing · organizing-schema

AIDA extended with Satisfaction to capture post-purchase loyalty.

AIDAS extends the classic AIDA framework by adding a fifth stage — Satisfaction — addressing the post-purchase phase that the original AIDA implicitly ended at Action (purchase). The Satisfaction stage recognizes that customer-relationship value extends beyond the initial transaction: a satisfied customer becomes a repeat purchaser, recommends the product to others (word-of-mouth, referral), and is less susceptible to competitor switching. The extension is conventionally attributed to selling-process literature in the mid-20th century, with the specific AIDAS acronym appearing in various selling textbooks from the 1960s onward, though the underlying recognition that selling continues after the sale was substantially developed by Theodore Levitt (Marketing Myopia, 1960) and the broader relationship-marketing tradition. AIDAS reflects the broader 20th-century shift from transactional to relationship-based marketing thinking. The framework retains AIDA's hierarchy-of-effects assumptions and inherits its critiques, but addresses the substantial real-world fact that marketing investments are best evaluated against customer lifetime value rather than initial-transaction conversion. Contemporary developments including Net Promoter Score, Customer Lifetime Value frameworks, and customer-success movements substantially extend AIDAS's post-purchase concerns into structured discipline.

Year / Decade

Primary sources

Various selling textbooks 1960s onward, broader relationship-marketing tradition including Levitt, T. (1960). 'Marketing Myopia', Harvard Business Review, Berry, L.L. (1983). 'Relationship Marketing' low

Core components

  • Original AIDA stages: Attention, Interest, Desire, Action
  • Fifth stage: Satisfaction (post-purchase customer experience and loyalty)
  • Connection to relationship marketing tradition
  • Recognition that selling continues after sale
  • Foundation for repeat-purchase and word-of-mouth thinking
  • Connection to Customer Lifetime Value frameworks
  • Connection to Net Promoter Score and customer-success movements

Primary use case

Sales-training and selling-process frameworks particularly in B2B contexts; basis for relationship-marketing thinking; foundation for some customer-success methodologies; reference framework in selling and customer-relationship-management literature; complement to AIDA in marketing education that addresses post-purchase phase; integration with customer-experience management.

Common criticisms

  • Inherits AIDA's hierarchy-of-effects critiques — sequential-cognitive assumptions challenged by empirical research
  • specific 'AIDAS' acronym attribution is vague — no single inventor with clear claim, and selling-textbook literature varies in which extension acronym they prefer (AIDAS, AIDCAS, AIDCRA all appear)
  • the framework treats Satisfaction as a stage rather than a continuing relationship dimension, which more sophisticated relationship-marketing frameworks address better
  • commercial selling-training industry has produced AIDAS as one of several similar mnemonic frameworks without clear empirical preference
  • 'Satisfaction' is operationally varied across implementations
  • the addition of one stage doesn't address AIDA's broader limitations about how marketing actually works
  • works principally in transactional-product contexts where 'satisfaction' has meaningful operational definition.
