Business Model Canvas

Also known as: BMC

framework · management · organizing-schema

Nine-block visual schema for describing and iterating a business model.

The Business Model Canvas is a visual one-page template developed by Alexander Osterwalder in his 2004 University of Lausanne doctoral dissertation and subsequently popularized in Business Model Generation (2010), co-authored with Yves Pigneur. The canvas organizes nine interrelated building blocks — Customer Segments, Value Propositions, Channels, Customer Relationships, Revenue Streams, Key Resources, Key Activities, Key Partnerships, and Cost Structure — that together describe how an organization creates, delivers, and captures value. The framework has become a near-universal standard tool in startup pitches, corporate innovation workshops, and entrepreneurship education, with substantial extensions including the Lean Canvas (Maurya's startup-focused adaptation) and the Value Proposition Canvas (Strategyzer's zoom-in on the value-proposition and customer-segment blocks).

Common criticisms

  • Descriptive snapshot rather than analytical or strategic tool — doesn't capture competitive dynamics, defensibility, or trajectory
  • flattens fundamentally different business types into one template
  • weak on platform, network-effect, and multi-sided businesses where 'customer segments' is genuinely multi-sided
  • treats blocks as independent when they are deeply interlinked
  • can become a workshop artifact rather than driving real decisions
  • doesn't surface critical assumptions or risks the way some competitors (e.g., Lean Canvas) do.
