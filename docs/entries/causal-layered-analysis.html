Causal Layered Analysis

Also known as: CLA

framework · management · organizing-schema

Sohail Inayatullah's four-layer futures methodology analyzing the litany, social causes, worldviews, and myths underlying any contested future.

Causal Layered Analysis (CLA) is the critical-futures-studies methodology developed by Pakistani-Australian futures scholar Sohail Inayatullah, articulated in his 1998 article 'Causal Layered Analysis: Post-structuralism as Method' in Futures and consolidated in his 2002 book Questioning the Future: Methods and Tools for Organizational and Societal Transformation (multiple editions). CLA decomposes any futures issue into four increasingly deep layers of analysis: (1) Litany — the surface phenomena, headline-news, popular discourse, official statistics; (2) Systemic causes — social, economic, political, technological systems producing the litany; (3) Worldview — the underlying paradigm, ideology, cultural assumptions; (4) Myth/metaphor — the deep narrative, mythic structure, root metaphor constituting the issue at the most fundamental level. CLA draws on poststructuralist theory (Foucault's discourse analysis, Edward Said's orientalism critique), critical social theory, and futures-studies tradition (notably Wendell Bell's substantive work). The method is used principally for transformative futures inquiry challenging surface-level forecasting.

Core components

  • Four layers of analysis (CLA's central structure): (1) Litany — surface phenomena: headlines, official statistics, popular discourse, the 'visible' face of the issue
  • (2) Systemic causes — social, economic, political, technological, environmental systems producing the litany
  • cause-and-effect at the structural level
  • (3) Worldview — discourse, paradigm, ideology, cultural assumptions, civilizational structure
  • the deeper view that structures what counts as systemic cause
  • (4) Myth/metaphor — the deep narrative, archetype, root metaphor, mythic structure constituting the issue at the foundational level
  • Methodological practice: identify how an issue appears at each layer
  • develop alternative futures by changing the deeper layers (worldview, myth) rather than only addressing surface phenomena
  • Transformative versus reformative change: CLA distinguishes change at the litany / systemic level (reform) from change at the worldview / myth level (transformation)
  • Multiple-civilizational-perspective integration: CLA explicitly engages non-Western worldviews and myths in futures inquiry, drawing on Indian, Islamic, Chinese, Confucian, indigenous philosophical traditions
  • Workshop methodology: group facilitation through the four layers using structured questions, narrative methods, image work
  • Application contexts: organizational futures, policy futures, civilizational futures, technological futures
  • Connection to other Inayatullah methods: Six Pillars of Futures Thinking, Futures Triangle, Macrohistory, Sarkar's Social Cycles application

Primary use case

Critical-futures-studies methodology for transformative futures inquiry; applied principally in: organizational strategic foresight (transformation-oriented); policy futures analysis; civilizational and cultural futures inquiry; international development and global-South futures (Inayatullah's substantial work in Pakistan, India, Iran, Malaysia, Australia); academic and professional reference in futures studies, critical theory, organizational transformation, and international-development literature; Tamkang University (Taiwan) Graduate Institute of Futures Studies, Metafuture (Inayatullah's consulting practice), UNESCO Futures Literacy Lab tradition; complementary methodology to scenario planning, Foresight Cone, backcasting, and other futures methods; intellectual foundation for transformative-futures-literacy practice and post-Western futures studies; pedagogical reference in critical-futures-studies graduate curricula globally.

Common criticisms

  • CLA's poststructuralist foundations have been substantially debated — empirical-futures-studies advocates argue the method's emphasis on worldview and myth produces analytical depth at the cost of predictive precision and decision-relevance, with analyses of mythic structure not translating clearly to operational decisions
  • the four-layer structure is methodologically convenient but the boundaries between layers (where systemic cause ends and worldview begins) are substantively undefined and depend on analyst judgment
  • reproducibility is limited — different analysts applying CLA to the same issue produce different layer-content with limited convergence, raising questions about the method's analytical rigor
  • the explicit engagement with non-Western worldviews is methodologically valuable but the practical operationalization in facilitated workshops requires substantial cultural competence that few practitioners possess, producing facile cross-cultural references that may misrepresent the source traditions
  • integration with quantitative forecasting and decision-analysis techniques is methodologically underdeveloped — CLA operates in a different register from probabilistic forecasting, with practical synthesis difficult
  • Inayatullah's sometimes-substantial inclusion of P.R. Sarkar's social-cycles theory and other specific spiritual-philosophical content has been argued by some commentators to embed particular worldview claims in what is presented as methodological framework
  • the transformation-versus-reformation distinction has been critiqued as binary — substantive change frequently combines elements of both rather than purely operating at one level
  • commercial-consulting context (Inayatullah's substantial Metafuture consulting practice, workshop fees, training programs) raises ordinary concerns about institutional incentives shaping framework promotion
  • reception in mainstream Western strategic-foresight practice has been limited despite substantial reception in academic critical-futures-studies and global-South policy contexts.
