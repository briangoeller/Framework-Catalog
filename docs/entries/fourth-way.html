Fourth Way

Also known as: Gurdjieff Work

framework · theology · esoteric-tradition

Gurdjieff and Ouspensky's framework integrating self-remembering, the three centers, and the enneagram of process (distinct from the personality enneagram) toward conscious development.

The Fourth Way is the spiritual teaching developed by Greek-Armenian mystic G.I. Gurdjieff (c. 1866-1877 birth — exact date contested — to 1949) and articulated principally through his student P.D. Ouspensky's posthumously-published In Search of the Miraculous (1949) and Gurdjieff's own All and Everything trilogy (Beelzebub's Tales to His Grandson 1950, Meetings with Remarkable Men 1963, Life Is Real Only Then When 'I Am' 1975, all published posthumously). The system is named the 'Fourth Way' in distinction from the three traditional ways of spiritual development — the fakir's way (working with the body), the monk's way (working with emotion), and the yogi's way (working with intellect) — which Gurdjieff characterized as requiring withdrawal from ordinary life. The Fourth Way works in ordinary life, integrating physical, emotional, and intellectual development. Central Gurdjieffian concepts include the claim that man is mechanical and asleep, the necessity of self-remembering and self-observation, the Enneagram as cosmological diagram, and sacred dances (Movements) as embodied spiritual practice.

Originators

G.I. Gurdjieff (c. 1866/1877-1949, foundational and principal teacher); Pyotr Demianovich Ouspensky (1878-1947, principal student and systematic articulator through In Search of the Miraculous); Maurice Nicoll (1884-1953, Jungian psychiatrist and Gurdjieff student, Psychological Commentaries on the Teaching of Gurdjieff and Ouspensky 1952-1956); Jeanne de Salzmann (1889-1990, Gurdjieff's principal lieutenant and successor, founded the Gurdjieff Foundation); intellectual antecedents claimed by Gurdjieff in Sufi mysticism, Tibetan Buddhism, ancient Egyptian religion, Christian Orthodox monasticism, the 'Sarmoung Brotherhood' (a claimed Central Asian esoteric school whose historical existence is substantially contested) high

Year / Decade

Pre-1912 (Gurdjieff's reported Central Asian travels and teaching preparation); 1915 onward (teaching in Russia); 1922-1933 (Institute for the Harmonious Development of Man, Fontainebleau-Avon); 1949 (Gurdjieff's death); 1950 onward (publication and successor-organization development) medium

Band notes

Gurdjieff's source claims (Sarmoung Brotherhood) are unverifiable; the system's effectiveness claims rest on practitioner testimony rather than empirical evaluation.

Core components

  • Three traditional ways plus the Fourth Way: way of fakir (body), way of monk (emotion), way of yogi (intellect), Fourth Way (integration in ordinary life)
  • Man as machine: claim that ordinary human consciousness is mechanical, reactive, and asleep
  • Self-remembering: practice of dual attention (attention to the present moment and simultaneous attention to oneself as observer)
  • Self-observation: non-judgmental witnessing of one's own mechanical reactions
  • Three centers: intellectual, emotional, moving (with instinctive and sex centers as additional categories in later articulations)
  • Three lines of work: work on oneself, work with others, work for the school
  • The Work: comprehensive term for the Fourth Way method
  • Enneagram: nine-pointed cosmological diagram, claimed ancient origin and central pedagogical importance (distinguished from the substantially distinct modern Enneagram of Personality which derived from Oscar Ichazo and Claudio Naranjo's mid-20th-century synthesis)
  • Sacred Dances (Movements): codified physical sequences claimed to encode spiritual-cosmological information
  • Octave / Law of Seven and Triad / Law of Three: cosmological structures Gurdjieff used to describe process and reality
  • All and Everything: Gurdjieff's substantial textual legacy including Beelzebub's Tales (deliberately written in obscure style as pedagogical device)

Primary use case

Spiritual teaching for Fourth Way / Gurdjieff Work communities globally; principal organizations: Gurdjieff Foundation (founded by Jeanne de Salzmann, with branches in Paris, London, New York, San Francisco, Caracas, others), Nicoll groups, Ouspensky-derived lineages, independent Fourth Way communities; intellectual influence on subsequent Western esotericism, transpersonal psychology, and consciousness studies; subject of substantial academic scholarship including James Webb's The Harmonious Circle (1980, the standard critical-historical study), Joseph Azize's recent academic engagement (Gurdjieff: Mysticism, Contemplation, and Exercises 2020), and Sophia Wellbeloved's scholarship; literary and cultural reception (J.G. Bennett's substantial Gurdjieffian writing; Peter Brook's 1979 film Meetings with Remarkable Men; cultural references in Doris Lessing, Katherine Mansfield, Frank Lloyd Wright); the modern Enneagram of Personality is substantially distinct from Gurdjieff's Enneagram despite shared diagram and disputed-genealogical claims.

Common criticisms

  • Gurdjieff's biographical claims have been substantially contested by scholarship — his reports of extensive travels in Central Asia, encounters with the Sarmoung Brotherhood, and acquisition of ancient esoteric teachings are largely undocumented and James Webb's The Harmonious Circle (1980) substantially demonstrated that Gurdjieff's autobiographical narratives mix verifiable history with substantially unverifiable claims, making his claimed access to ancient esoteric sources difficult to assess
  • the charisma-based teaching produced documented cult-of-personality dynamics — Gurdjieff's Institute for the Harmonious Development of Man at the Prieuré (Fontainebleau-Avon, 1922-1933) was characterized by the New York Times and other contemporary press as involving extreme work demands, financial-control dynamics with wealthy students (Lady Rothermere, Lord Pentland), and Gurdjieff's deliberate provocations toward students (the 'tooska' / shocks methodology)
  • the Movements and the Enneagram have been argued by scholars to be Gurdjieff's own innovations rather than ancient transmissions, with the Sarmoung-source claim lacking historical corroboration
  • substantial intra-tradition disputes about lineage authority — Gurdjieff Foundation (de Salzmann lineage), Nicoll groups, Bennett-derived lineages (the Bennett-de Salzmann split), Ouspensky-derived lineages, and independent communities all claim authentic Fourth Way transmission
  • the Gurdjieff Foundation's centralization of authority and gatekeeping around publication of Movements and certain teachings has produced intra-tradition controversy
  • substantial concerns about the Foundation's governance and intellectual-property control of Gurdjieff's literary estate
  • the deliberately-obscure prose style of Beelzebub's Tales (Gurdjieff stated his intent to 'destroy mercilessly' preconceived ideas in his readers) produces substantial interpretive variation across Fourth Way communities
  • modern commercial appropriation of 'the Enneagram' (particularly the Enneagram of Personality through Helen Palmer, Don Riso, Russ Hudson, Beatrice Chestnut) has produced consumer products substantially disconnected from Gurdjieff's original teaching.
