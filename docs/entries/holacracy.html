Holacracy

framework · management · organizing-schema

Self-management system replacing traditional hierarchy with distributed authority and circles.

Holacracy is a structured self-management system developed by Brian Robertson and Tom Thomason at Ternary Software in the mid-2000s and codified in the Holacracy Constitution and Robertson's book Holacracy (2015). It replaces traditional management hierarchy with a system of nested 'circles' (work teams) containing roles defined by purpose, accountabilities, and domains, with authority distributed to roles rather than people. Two distinctive governance processes — Tactical Meetings (handling operational tensions) and Governance Meetings (modifying roles, accountabilities, and policies through integrative decision-making) — operate under highly structured Robert's-Rules-like procedures. Zappos's high-profile 2014 adoption (and later partial unwind) was the most prominent corporate implementation; subsequent academic and practitioner analyses have produced mixed results, with substantial implementation challenges around the steep learning curve and discomfort with codified governance among employees.

Originators

Brian Robertson; Tom Thomason high

Year / Decade

Mid-2000s development; 2010 (Holacracy Constitution); 2015 (Robertson book) high

Primary sources

Robertson, B.J. (2015). Holacracy: The New Management System for a Rapidly Changing World, HolacracyOne. The Holacracy Constitution (multiple versions, 2010 onward) high

Core components

  • Holacracy Constitution
  • Circles (nested work units)
  • Roles with purpose, accountabilities, domains
  • Distributed authority (to roles, not people)
  • Tactical Meetings (operational tensions)
  • Governance Meetings (role and policy changes)
  • Integrative Decision-Making
  • Tension processing
  • Lead Link and Rep Link circle roles

Primary use case

Self-management implementation in companies seeking to replace traditional hierarchy; foundation for some 'Teal' organization implementations (Laloux); software implementations like GlassFrog tooling; alternative governance for purpose-driven organizations.

Common criticisms

  • Steep learning curve and high cognitive overhead
  • Zappos's well-publicized partial unwind raised questions about scalability
  • codified governance language and procedures can feel alienating and bureaucratic
  • trades implicit hierarchy for explicit governance complexity, which may not be a net improvement
  • resistance from employees who prefer clear management
  • works better in small, ideologically-aligned organizations than at scale
  • commercial control of the brand and certification by HolacracyOne creates barriers to independent adaptation.
