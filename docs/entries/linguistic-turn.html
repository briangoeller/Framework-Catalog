Linguistic Turn

framework · history · philosophical-tradition

Hayden White's and others' framework treating historical writing as fundamentally narrative construction shaped by rhetorical and linguistic choices.

The Linguistic Turn in history is the methodological reorientation, primarily from the 1970s onward, treating historical writing as fundamentally narrative construction shaped by rhetorical, linguistic, and tropological choices rather than transparent representation of past reality. The term itself is borrowed from Richard Rorty's edited volume The Linguistic Turn: Essays in Philosophical Method (1967, addressing philosophy not history), but its application to history descends from Hayden White's Metahistory: The Historical Imagination in Nineteenth-Century Europe (1973) and Tropics of Discourse (1978), Dominick LaCapra's work on intellectual history and historical writing, Roger Chartier's cultural-history extensions, and Joan Scott's gender-history reformulation ('Gender: A Useful Category of Historical Analysis' 1986). The Linguistic Turn substantially reshaped intellectual history, cultural history, and women's and gender history through the 1980s-90s before partially receding as historical practice integrated some of its insights without adopting its strongest claims.

Originators

Hayden White (foundational for history, Metahistory 1973, Tropics of Discourse 1978); Dominick LaCapra (Rethinking Intellectual History 1983); Joan Scott ('Gender: A Useful Category of Historical Analysis' 1986); Roger Chartier (cultural history extensions); intellectual antecedents in Richard Rorty (Linguistic Turn 1967), structuralist and poststructuralist literary theory (Barthes, Derrida, Foucault), Frank Ankersmit's narrativist philosophy high

1967 (Rorty volume in philosophy); 1973 (White's Metahistory canonical for history); 1980s-1990s (peak influence) high

Core components

  • Treatment of historical writing as narrative construction rather than transparent representation
  • Attention to rhetorical, linguistic, and tropological choices in historical writing
  • Discourse analysis as historical methodology
  • Critique of empiricist historiography's claim to objective representation
  • Hayden White's master tropes (metaphor, metonymy, synecdoche, irony) governing historical emplotment
  • Modes of emplotment (romance, comedy, tragedy, satire) and ideological implication
  • Joan Scott's reconceptualization of gender as discursive category
  • Foucauldian discourse analysis of historical-knowledge production
  • Critique of binary oppositions and master narratives

Primary use case

Methodological foundation for narrative-focused intellectual and cultural history; research framework for gender history, women's history, history of sexuality; intellectual foundation for adjacent fields including cultural studies, literary history, history of science (David Bloor, Steven Shapin); academic reference in graduate-level historiography; engagement with broader debates about history's epistemological claims and its relationship to literary studies.

Common criticisms

  • The Linguistic Turn has been substantially contested across multiple fronts — empirical-historical scholars argued the strongest claims (historical writing as 'mere narrative') threatened to dissolve history's evidentiary discipline, with Bryan Palmer's Descent into Discourse (1990) providing influential Marxist critique from the left
  • the application of Hayden White's tropological framework to Holocaust history generated particularly acute debate (Carlo Ginzburg, Saul Friedländer, Geoffrey Roberts), with critics arguing the framework's treatment of historical writing as narrative-equivalent risked undermining the truth-claim distinction between Holocaust history and Holocaust denial
  • Richard Evans's In Defence of History (1997) provided an influential summary defense of historical empiricism against the Linguistic Turn's strongest claims
  • Joan Scott's gender-discourse framework was challenged within feminist history by social-historians arguing it dematerialized class and economic conditions of women's lives
  • the partial reception of Linguistic Turn insights into mainstream historical practice (with empirical work continuing while narrative-construction awareness was incorporated) has been argued by some scholars as either progressive integration or dilution of the tradition's radical commitments
  • cross-tradition reception varied, with German historiography substantially engaging through Begriffsgeschichte while remaining methodologically distinct
  • recent history-of-emotions, cognitive-history, and material-history work has developed methodologies that incorporate post-Linguistic-Turn narrative-awareness while reconnecting to non-discursive evidence.
