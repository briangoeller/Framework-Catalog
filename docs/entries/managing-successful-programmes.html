Managing Successful Programmes

Also known as: MSP

framework · project management · regulatory-standard

AXELOS programme management methodology for delivering transformational change through coordinated projects.

Managing Successful Programmes (MSP) is the AXELOS programme management methodology for delivering transformational change through coordinated portfolios of projects. Originally developed by the UK Office of Government Commerce (OGC) and first published in 1999, with subsequent editions in 2003, 2007, 2011, and the fifth edition in 2020, MSP distinguishes programme management (delivering benefits and outcomes through coordinated change) from project management (delivering outputs to specification, time, and cost). MSP's central commitments are vision-led delivery, benefit-driven investment, end-state design through a Blueprint, and tranches-based incremental delivery. The fifth edition (2020) substantially restructured the framework around principles, themes, and processes paralleling PRINCE2's structural pattern. MSP is widely used in UK central and local government transformation programmes and in similarly-scaled corporate change initiatives internationally.

Core components

  • Seven principles (lead with purpose, collaborate across boundaries, deal with ambiguity, align with priorities, deploy diverse skills, realise measurable benefits, bring pace and value)
  • Seven themes (organisation, design, justification, structure, knowledge, assurance, decisions)
  • Seven processes (identifying a programme, designing the outcomes, planning progressive delivery, delivering the capabilities, embedding the outcomes, evaluating new information, closing)
  • Vision Statement, Blueprint, Benefits Realisation Plan as central management products
  • Tranches-based incremental delivery
  • Senior Responsible Owner role

Primary use case

Programme management methodology for UK central government transformation programmes (DWP, NHS, MoD, HMRC, etc.) and local government strategic change; international adoption in AXELOS-aligned jurisdictions; private-sector use particularly in financial services and large corporates undergoing strategic change; basis for the MSP Foundation and Practitioner certifications; reference framework for benefit-led investment cases in regulated sectors.

Common criticisms

  • MSP has been criticized along with the broader programme management literature for framework heaviness disproportionate to delivered benefit — academic researchers including Pellegrinelli, Maylor, and Thiry have argued the programme-management literature in general overpromises predictability in transformational-change contexts where outcomes are inherently uncertain
  • UK National Audit Office and Public Accounts Committee reports across the 2010s documented persistent failures in MSP-aligned government programmes (notably IT and welfare-reform programmes), suggesting the methodology has not translated into reliable delivery
  • the SRO role concentrates accountability in ways that political-cycle leadership change makes fragile
  • benefits-realisation tracking is empirically rare in completed programmes despite methodological centrality, an issue documented in NAO and academic studies
  • the 2020 fifth-edition restructuring produced transition difficulty for trainers and certified practitioners
  • commercial AXELOS / PeopleCert pricing has been contested
  • integration with PRINCE2 at the project level and with portfolio management above is articulated in principle but operationally awkward in many implementations.
