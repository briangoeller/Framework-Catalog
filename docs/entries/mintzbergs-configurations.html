Mintzberg's Configurations

framework · management · organizing-schema

Five organizational structures from simple to adhocracy, defined by coordination mechanism.

Henry Mintzberg's organizational configurations, presented in The Structuring of Organizations (1979) and refined in Structure in Fives (1983), characterize organizations along five basic configurations defined by the dominant coordination mechanism, key part of the organization, and design parameters. Simple Structure (direct supervision, strategic apex, e.g., entrepreneurial firm); Machine Bureaucracy (standardization of work processes, technostructure, e.g., mass producer); Professional Bureaucracy (standardization of skills, operating core, e.g., hospital, university); Divisionalized Form (standardization of outputs, middle line, e.g., diversified conglomerate); Adhocracy (mutual adjustment, support staff, e.g., consulting firm, R&D lab). Mintzberg later added Missionary (standardization of norms) and Political configurations. The framework provides a configurational alternative to contingency theory's variable-by-variable analysis, arguing that effective organizations achieve internal consistency among their structural elements.

Primary sources

Core components

  • Five configurations: Simple Structure, Machine Bureaucracy, Professional Bureaucracy, Divisionalized Form, Adhocracy
  • Six coordination mechanisms (direct supervision, mutual adjustment, standardization of work processes, outputs, skills, norms)
  • Five basic parts of the organization (strategic apex, middle line, operating core, technostructure, support staff)
  • Configurational hypothesis (consistency over piecemeal optimization)
  • Later additions of Missionary and Political configurations

Primary use case

Organizational design diagnosis; teaching framework in organizational theory; basis for analyzing fit among structural elements; reference for thinking about which configuration fits which environmental and strategic context.

Common criticisms

  • Configurational hypothesis empirically supported in some studies, contested in others
  • modern organizations often blend configurations rather than fitting one cleanly
  • framework predates platform organizations, networked enterprises, and remote-first structures
  • pure types may be increasingly rare
  • less prescriptive than diagnostic
  • some configurations (Adhocracy) are aspirational labels rather than empirically common forms.
