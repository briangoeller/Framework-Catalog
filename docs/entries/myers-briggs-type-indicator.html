Myers-Briggs Type Indicator

Also known as: MBTI

framework · psychology · organizing-schema

Sixteen personality types from Jungian dichotomies; widely used despite weak psychometric support.

The MBTI is a personality typology classifying individuals into one of sixteen types from four dichotomies (Extraversion/Introversion, Sensing/Intuition, Thinking/Feeling, Judging/Perceiving). Developed by Isabel Briggs Myers and her mother Katharine Cook Briggs from Carl Jung's typological theory, it became enormously popular in corporate training and team-building contexts. Mainstream personality psychology rejects it on psychometric grounds — its forced-choice dichotomies impose categorical structure on continuously distributed traits, test-retest reliability is poor, and predictive validity beyond chance has not been established.

Weak psychometric reliability and validity; rejected by mainstream personality psychology despite cultural penetration. Bimodal forced-choice on continuously distributed traits.

Core components

  • Extraversion/Introversion
  • Sensing/Intuition
  • Thinking/Feeling
  • Judging/Perceiving
  • sixteen resulting types

Primary use case

Corporate team-building, career counseling, and self-development contexts; use in high-stakes decisions like hiring is generally discouraged by mainstream psychology.

Common criticisms

  • Forced-choice dichotomies misrepresent continuous trait distributions
  • weak test-retest reliability with many people changing types on retest
  • lack of empirical validation for the sixteen-type structure
  • rejected by mainstream personality psychology in favor of dimensional models like the Big Five.
