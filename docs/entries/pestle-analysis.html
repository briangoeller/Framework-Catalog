PESTLE Analysis ⚑

Also known as: PEST, PESTEL, STEEPLE

tool · management · organizing-schema

Scans Political, Economic, Social, Technological, Legal, and Environmental factors.

PESTLE Analysis is a strategic environmental-scanning tool that organizes external macro-environmental factors into six categories: Political (government policy, political stability), Economic (growth, inflation, exchange rates, interest rates), Social (demographics, culture, attitudes), Technological (R&D activity, automation, technology incentives), Legal (employment, consumer protection, antitrust), and Environmental (weather, climate, ecology). Earlier variants — PEST (four factors), PESTEL (six), STEEP, STEEPLE (adding Ethical) — represent successive expansions. The tool is generally attributed to Francis Aguilar's Scanning the Business Environment (1967), where ETPS (Economic, Technical, Political, Social) was the original framing. PESTLE complements SWOT (which is firm-centric) by ensuring the external scan is structured rather than ad hoc, and is widely used in strategic planning, market entry analysis, and macroeconomic risk assessment.

Core components

  • Political factors
  • Economic factors
  • Social factors
  • Technological factors
  • Legal factors
  • Environmental factors
  • Variants: PEST (4), PESTEL/PESTLE (6), STEEPLE (7 with Ethical)
  • External macro-environmental scan
  • Often paired with SWOT

Primary use case

Strategic planning environmental scanning; market entry analysis; macroeconomic risk assessment; structured external context analysis in business plans and consulting engagements; teaching tool in strategy courses.

Common criticisms

  • Like SWOT, generates lists rather than analysis without prioritization or causal linkage
  • categorization of any specific factor across categories is often arbitrary (e.g., is a carbon tax Political, Economic, Legal, or Environmental?)
  • static snapshot of fast-moving environments
  • doesn't address inter-factor dynamics
  • can become checkbox compliance rather than genuine scanning
  • many practitioners use it as a brainstorming starter without rigorous research backing
  • environmental scans are only as good as the underlying research, which the framework doesn't compel.
