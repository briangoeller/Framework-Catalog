Pixar Story Spine

framework · narrative studies · organizing-schema

Once upon a time / every day / one day / because of that / until finally / and ever since then.

The Pixar Story Spine is the narrative-development framework using a fill-in-the-blank sentence structure: 'Once upon a time _____. Every day, _____. One day _____. Because of that, _____. Because of that, _____. Until finally _____. And ever since then, _____.' The framework was substantially developed by improv theater pioneer Kenn Adams in the 1990s for improv-based storytelling exercises and was substantially popularized through Pixar story-development practice — particularly through former Pixar story artist Emma Coats's 2011-2012 'Pixar's 22 Rules of Storytelling' Twitter thread, where the Story Spine appeared as one of the rules and was substantially attributed (mistakenly) to Pixar rather than Adams. The framework's appeal lies in its pedagogical clarity and its substantial alignment with classical narrative structure: 'Once upon a time' establishes setting and protagonist; 'Every day' establishes status quo and protagonist's normal life; 'One day' provides inciting incident disrupting the status quo; 'Because of that... Because of that' chains causal consequences (the rising action of three-act structure); 'Until finally' provides climactic resolution; 'And ever since then' establishes new equilibrium showing transformation. The framework has been substantially adopted in narrative-design education across film, animation, advertising, marketing, education, and substantial commercial storytelling-craft training. The Story Spine works well as introductory tool for understanding causal narrative structure and for developing story premises before substantial elaboration. Critics note that the framework's clean causal chain ('because of that... because of that') may oversimplify narrative structure — many successful narratives involve substantial parallel plot threads, ensemble structure, or causal complexity that the linear spine doesn't capture. The misattribution to Pixar (the framework predates Pixar's adoption and was developed by Adams) demonstrates the credit-attribution challenges in story-craft methodology.

Originators

Kenn Adams (foundational, 1990s improv theater work, articulated in How to Improvise a Full-Length Play); subsequent Pixar adoption; Emma Coats (2011-2012 substantial popularization through 'Pixar's 22 Rules of Storytelling' Twitter thread) high

Year / Decade

1990s (Kenn Adams foundational); subsequent Pixar internal adoption; 2011-2012 (Coats substantial popularization) high

Primary sources

Adams, K. (2007). How to Improvise a Full-Length Play: The Art of Spontaneous Theater (foundational), Coats, E. (2011-2012). 'Pixar's 22 Rules of Storytelling' (Twitter thread popularization), subsequent storytelling-craft writing high

Core components

  • Seven-element fill-in-the-blank structure: Once upon a time / Every day / One day / Because of that / Because of that / Until finally / And ever since then
  • Causal chain emphasizing 'because of that' connections
  • Classical narrative-structure alignment (setup, status quo, inciting incident, rising action, climax, resolution)
  • Pedagogical clarity for introducing narrative structure
  • Substantial commercial adoption across film, animation, advertising, marketing, education
  • Original development by Adams for improv theater
  • Mistaken Pixar attribution through Coats popularization

Primary use case

Foundational pedagogical tool in narrative-development education; basis for substantial story-craft training across film, animation, advertising, marketing, business storytelling; reference framework in introductory creative writing; foundation for substantial commercial storytelling consulting and training; integration with broader narrative-structure tradition; pedagogical foundation for introducing causal narrative structure; influence on business and marketing storytelling; foundation for some game-narrative design work; basis for substantial Pixar internal story-development methodology.

Common criticisms

  • The framework's clean linear causal chain ('because of that... because of that') may oversimplify narrative structure — many successful narratives involve substantial parallel plot threads, ensemble structure, non-linear time, or causal complexity that the linear spine doesn't capture
  • commercial business-storytelling and marketing applications often produce template-driven content that hits the spine structure without substantive story quality
  • cross-genre application varies — works better for action and growth narratives than for atmosphere, character study, or experimental work
  • the framework's pedagogical accessibility (anyone can fill in seven blanks) sometimes produces superficial work rather than substantive storytelling
  • misattribution to Pixar (rather than to Kenn Adams's foundational improv work) demonstrates credit-attribution challenges that affect framework legitimacy
  • the framework works well as introductory tool but doesn't substantively guide elaboration into full narratives
  • integration with character development, theme, atmosphere is often weak
  • tendency for the framework to be applied as story-conformity check rather than as developmental tool
  • cross-cultural application of European fairy-tale 'once upon a time' framing has uneven results
  • commercial storytelling-craft industry has produced substantial adoption with varying analytical fidelity to the framework's substantive contribution.
