PMBOK Guide

Also known as: PMBOK; PMI Guide

framework · project management · regulatory-standard

PMI's Project Management Body of Knowledge defining processes, knowledge areas, and the basis for the PMP certification.

The PMBOK Guide (A Guide to the Project Management Body of Knowledge) is the principal publication of the Project Management Institute (PMI), articulating what PMI considers the generally recognized good practices of project management and serving as the basis for the Project Management Professional (PMP) certification examination. The first edition was published in 1996, with subsequent editions issued approximately every four years; the seventh edition (2021) substantially restructured the Guide from the prior process-and-knowledge-area architecture (47 processes across 10 knowledge areas in the sixth edition) to a principles-and-performance-domains architecture, incorporating predictive, hybrid, and adaptive (agile) lifecycle approaches. PMBOK is an ANSI standard (ANSI/PMI 99-001) and is referenced in many procurement and contracting frameworks. PMI also publishes companion standards for program management, portfolio management, and several specialized domains.

Core components

  • Seventh edition: 12 project management principles
  • 8 project performance domains (stakeholders, team, development approach and life cycle, planning, project work, delivery, measurement, uncertainty)
  • Tailoring guidance for predictive, hybrid, and adaptive approaches
  • Models, methods, and artifacts reference
  • Sixth-edition architecture (still widely referenced): 49 processes across 10 knowledge areas (integration, scope, schedule, cost, quality, resource, communications, risk, procurement, stakeholder)
  • Process groups (initiating, planning, executing, monitoring and controlling, closing)
  • Companion standards including The Standard for Program Management, The Standard for Portfolio Management, and The Standard for Risk Management

Primary use case

Reference framework for the PMP certification examination held by approximately 1.4 million certified individuals globally; basis for many corporate project-management methodologies and PMOs; input to government and contractor RFP and proposal evaluation criteria; academic reference in graduate-level project-management curricula; foundation for PMI's portfolio of derivative standards and certifications.

Common criticisms

  • PMBOK has been criticized across decades for prescriptive process orientation that the Standish Group's CHAOS reports and academic researchers including Bent Flyvbjerg argue does not correlate with project success — Flyvbjerg's research on megaproject performance (consistent cost overruns and schedule delays) is often cited against PMBOK-style method-following
  • the PMP certification's predictive validity for actual project performance has been challenged in studies including Starkweather and Stevenson 2011, which found weak correlations between certification and project outcomes
  • the 2021 seventh-edition shift to principles-and-performance-domains was partly a response to these critiques but produced practitioner confusion during transition and complicated PMP examination preparation
  • commercial training providers around PMP have created a substantial industry whose interests are not perfectly aligned with knowledge-base improvement
  • PMBOK underweights stakeholder politics, organizational power dynamics, and strategic context that empirical project-failure research consistently surfaces
  • integration with agile delivery has been slower than alternative frameworks, and Disciplined Agile acquisition (2019) has not fully resolved the predictive-vs-adaptive tension.
