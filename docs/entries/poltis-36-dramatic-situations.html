Polti's 36 Dramatic Situations

framework · narrative studies · organizing-schema

Catalog of underlying dramatic conflicts found across all stories.

Polti's 36 Dramatic Situations is the catalog of underlying dramatic conflicts articulated by French writer Georges Polti in his 1895 Les Trente-Six Situations Dramatiques (translated as The Thirty-Six Dramatic Situations 1916). Polti's foundational claim, citing earlier work by Carlo Gozzi and Goethe (Goethe reportedly told Schiller that Gozzi maintained 'there can be but thirty-six tragic situations' and Schiller, after working at the problem, concluded the same), was that all dramatic narratives can be analyzed into 36 fundamental conflict situations — combinations of moral elements creating dramatic tension that Polti claimed to have systematically catalogued through analysis of substantial body of dramatic literature. The 36 situations include: Supplication, Deliverance, Crime Pursued by Vengeance, Vengeance Taken for Kin Upon Kin, Pursuit, Disaster, Falling Prey to Cruelty/Misfortune, Revolt, Daring Enterprise, Abduction, The Enigma, Obtaining, Enmity of Kinsmen, Rivalry of Kinsmen, Murderous Adultery, Madness, Fatal Imprudence, Involuntary Crimes of Love, Slaying of a Kinsman Unrecognized, Self-Sacrificing for an Ideal, Self-Sacrifice for Kindred, All Sacrificed for a Passion, Necessity of Sacrificing Loved Ones, Rivalry of Superior and Inferior, Adultery, Crimes of Love, Discovery of the Dishonor of a Loved One, Obstacles to Love, An Enemy Loved, Ambition, Conflict with a God, Mistaken Jealousy, Erroneous Judgment, Remorse, Recovery of a Lost One, Loss of Loved Ones. Each situation specifies dramatic dynamic between roles (e.g., Persecutor and Persecuted in Supplication; Avenger and Criminal in Vengeance). The framework has been substantially influential in narrative analysis and creative writing education, providing analytical vocabulary for examining dramatic structure across genres and traditions. The 36-number specificity is substantially debated — Polti's enumeration involves substantial subjective categorization, and alternative dramatic-situation catalogs (Tobias's 20 Master Plots 1993, Booker's 7 Basic Plots 2004) provide substantively different enumerations. The framework's enduring appeal lies in its catalog form rather than its specific enumeration — the broader claim that recurring conflict patterns underlie diverse stories has substantial intuitive appeal even if the specific 36 are contested.

Originators

Georges Polti (foundational 1895); intellectual antecedents in Carlo Gozzi (18th century, original 36 claim attributed by Goethe); Goethe and Schiller's reported concurrence; subsequent narrative-analysis tradition high

Year / Decade

Late 18th century (Gozzi antecedent); 1895 (Polti foundational); 1916 (English translation); ongoing influence high

Primary sources

Polti, G. (1895, English translation 1916). Les Trente-Six Situations Dramatiques (The Thirty-Six Dramatic Situations), Tobias, R.B. (1993). 20 Master Plots and How to Build Them (alternative), Booker, C. (2004). The Seven Basic Plots: Why We Tell Stories (substantial alternative) high

Core components

  • Catalog of 36 dramatic situations
  • Each situation as combination of dramatic roles and conflict dynamics
  • Foundation in claim that recurring conflict patterns underlie all stories
  • Connection to Carlo Gozzi (18th century) and Goethe-Schiller intellectual tradition
  • Application to narrative analysis across genres and traditions
  • Distinction from but parallel to other dramatic-situation catalogs (Tobias 20 Master Plots, Booker 7 Basic Plots)
  • Substantial influence on creative-writing education
  • Pedagogical durability across narrative-studies curricula

Primary use case

Foundational catalog framework in narrative analysis; basis for substantial dramatic-situation analysis across film, theater, novels, games; reference framework in creative-writing education; foundation for some commercial story-development methodology; integration with broader narrative theory; pedagogical reference in narrative-studies curricula; influence on screenwriting craft as analytical vocabulary; basis for some computational narrative analysis (categorizing stories by Polti situation).

Common criticisms

  • The 36-number specificity is substantially debated — Polti's enumeration involves substantial subjective categorization, with substantial overlap among situations and unclear principled basis for choosing 36 rather than other numbers
  • alternative dramatic-situation catalogs (Ronald Tobias's 20 Master Plots 1993, Christopher Booker's 7 Basic Plots 2004) provide substantively different enumerations, raising questions about which is correct and on what basis
  • the framework's late-19th-century European origins reflect particular cultural assumptions about dramatic conflict that may not transfer cross-culturally — non-Western narrative traditions may have substantially different conflict-pattern structures that Polti's catalog doesn't capture
  • commercial creative-writing education sometimes uses Polti's catalog as conformity check rather than as analytical tool
  • tendency for Polti analysis to produce categorization rather than substantive narrative improvement — knowing a story's Polti situation doesn't substantially help write it better
  • the framework's catalog form (each situation distinct) understates how successful stories often combine multiple Polti situations or substantially modify them
  • integration with character development, theme, atmosphere is often weak
  • cross-genre application varies — works better for traditional dramatic genres (tragedy, melodrama, romance) than for satire, comedy of manners, experimental work, ensemble pieces
  • the framework's pedagogical durability sometimes exceeds its current analytical force.
