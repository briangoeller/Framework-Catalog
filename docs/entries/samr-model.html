SAMR Model ⚑

framework · education · organizing-schema

Puentedura's four levels of technology integration: substitution, augmentation, modification, redefinition.

The SAMR Model was developed by Ruben Puentedura in the early 2010s as a framework for evaluating how technology is being used in instruction, articulating four progressive levels of integration. Substitution: technology acts as a direct substitute for traditional tools with no functional change (typing instead of handwriting; reading a PDF instead of a printed book). Augmentation: technology substitutes with functional improvement (digital text with embedded definitions; word-processing with spell-check). Modification: technology allows significant task redesign (collaborative document editing; multimedia presentations replacing essay submissions). Redefinition: technology enables previously inconceivable tasks (global student collaboration; immediate worldwide audience for student work). The first two levels represent enhancement; the second two represent transformation. The framework's analytical force is in distinguishing meaningful technology use from technology-as-fancy-substitute, with the implication that transformation-level integration produces greater learning benefit than substitution-level. SAMR has been substantially adopted in K-12 educational-technology professional development and is widely cited alongside TPACK as complementary frameworks. The empirical foundation for the specific four-level hierarchy and its claimed learning benefits is limited — Puentedura's published work is principally consultancy and presentation materials rather than peer-reviewed research, and the framework's iconic status in educational-technology professional development outpaces its empirical validation.

Core components

  • Four levels: Substitution, Augmentation, Modification, Redefinition
  • Two phases: Enhancement (S, A) and Transformation (M, R)
  • Progressive integration toward transformation
  • Distinction from technology-as-fancy-substitute
  • Often paired with TPACK in educational-technology professional development
  • Application to evaluating specific technology uses in instruction

Primary use case

K-12 educational-technology professional development; foundation for many district technology-integration initiatives; teacher reflection and self-assessment tool for technology use; complement to TPACK in tech-integration training; reference framework in educational-technology coaching; basis for some technology-integration evaluation rubrics.

Common criticisms

  • Empirical foundation for the specific four-level hierarchy is limited — Puentedura's published work is principally consultancy and presentation materials rather than peer-reviewed research
  • the implicit hierarchy (Redefinition is best) has been argued to undervalue substitution and augmentation when they're appropriate to task and context
  • classification of specific technology uses into SAMR levels is subjective with substantial inter-rater variability
  • can become a checklist driving teachers toward technology use that isn't pedagogically justified
  • commercial certification and consulting industry has shaped adoption
  • framework's apparent simplicity obscures genuine complexity in evaluating technology integration
  • works better as conversation-starter for technology integration than as rigorous evaluation tool
  • alternatives including the TIM (Technology Integration Matrix) offer more structured evaluation.
