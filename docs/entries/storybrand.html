StoryBrand

Also known as: SB7

framework · marketing · organizing-schema

Donald Miller's seven-part brand-narrative framework casting customer as hero, brand as guide.

StoryBrand (or the SB7 Framework) was developed by Donald Miller and presented in Building a StoryBrand: Clarify Your Message So Customers Will Listen (2017). The framework applies narrative structure (substantially drawing on Joseph Campbell's hero's-journey monomyth as popularized by Christopher Vogler's The Writer's Journey) to marketing communications, structured around seven elements: (1) A Character (the customer, framed as the hero of the story); (2) Has a Problem (external problem, internal feelings, philosophical stakes); (3) Meets a Guide (the brand, positioned as helpful guide rather than as the hero); (4) Who Gives Them a Plan (process plan, agreement plan); (5) And Calls Them to Action (direct call, transitional call); (6) That Helps Them Avoid Failure (stakes if they don't act); (7) And Ends in Success (concrete vision of resolved life). Miller's central commitment is that marketing communications should make the customer the hero and the brand the guide — flipping the common pattern where the brand presents itself as the hero of its own story. StoryBrand has been substantially commercialized through Miller's StoryBrand consultancy, certified-guide network, and live-event business. The framework has gained widespread adoption in small-business and B2B marketing contexts, though it has drawn substantial critique for being more formulaic than its narrative-craft origins suggest and for producing template-driven marketing copy that may underweight category-specific marketing principles.

Originators

Donald Miller (StoryBrand articulation); intellectual antecedents in Joseph Campbell's Hero's Journey monomyth, Christopher Vogler's The Writer's Journey high

Year / Decade

2017 (Building a StoryBrand); ongoing commercial development high

Primary sources

Miller, D. (2017). Building a StoryBrand: Clarify Your Message So Customers Will Listen, Miller, D. (2021). Marketing Made Simple, Campbell, J. (1949). The Hero with a Thousand Faces (intellectual antecedent), Vogler, C. (1992). The Writer's Journey high

Core components

  • Seven framework elements: Character, Problem, Guide, Plan, Call to Action, Failure (avoided), Success
  • Customer as hero, brand as guide
  • Three problem dimensions: external, internal, philosophical
  • Two plan types: process plan, agreement plan
  • Two CTA types: direct, transitional
  • Narrative-structure foundation in Campbell-Vogler hero's journey
  • Commercial certified-guide network
  • BrandScript document as canonical artifact
  • StoryBrand Marketing Workshop and consulting business

Primary use case

Small-business marketing copywriting; B2B marketing message development; website and landing-page structure; sales-pitch development; reference framework in faith-based and values-driven business communities (Miller's audience originates partly in Christian-business contexts); foundation for many freelance copywriting and marketing-consulting practices; basis for the StoryBrand certified-guide network business model.

Common criticisms

  • Substantially template-driven application can produce homogeneous marketing copy across StoryBrand-trained agencies and consultancies
  • Ehrenberg-Bass empirical research challenges the assumption that distinctive narrative messaging drives brand growth (penetration through mental and physical availability matters more than message differentiation)
  • the customer-as-hero framing oversimplifies — many real purchases don't fit hero's-journey structure (commodity purchases, habit-driven purchases, low-involvement decisions)
  • commercial certified-guide industry has substantial financial incentive to produce StoryBrand applications regardless of fit with specific business contexts
  • the framework works principally for considered-purchase contexts where storytelling has room to operate, less well for everyday-product or commodity contexts
  • Miller's evangelical-Christian audience and framing has produced substantial overlap between StoryBrand and Christian-business movements that has not always traveled well to secular contexts
  • the formulaic 'fill in the BrandScript' application can substitute for genuine customer insight
  • underweights category-specific marketing principles (B2B differs from B2C, services from products, etc.).
