Structural Anthropology

framework · anthropology · organizing-schema

Lévi-Strauss' framework analyzing culture through binary oppositions and underlying structures.

Structural Anthropology is the substantial framework articulated by French anthropologist Claude Lévi-Strauss from the 1940s onward (substantial Structural Anthropology 1958 collected essays, with foundational earlier work including The Elementary Structures of Kinship 1949), applying Saussurean structural linguistics (separately enriched in linguistics batch) to cultural analysis. Lévi-Strauss's central commitments include: (1) cultural phenomena (kinship systems, myths, classification systems, rituals) can be analyzed as systems of differences operating through binary oppositions (raw/cooked, nature/culture, male/female, life/death) — the same Saussurean differential logic Lévi-Strauss applied to language; (2) underlying structures of human mind produce substantial cross-cultural commonalities despite substantial surface variation — humans organize cultural meaning through universal cognitive operations; (3) myths in particular have logical structure that can be revealed through systematic analysis of mythemes (basic units) and their transformations across versions and cultures; (4) the structuralist project seeks the underlying grammar of cultural phenomena rather than their surface variability. Lévi-Strauss's substantial body of work includes The Elementary Structures of Kinship (1949) substantially analyzing kinship through exchange theory, Tristes Tropiques (1955) substantial intellectual memoir, Structural Anthropology (1958, second volume 1973), The Savage Mind (1962), and the four-volume Mythologiques (1964-1971) substantially analyzing American Indigenous mythology. The framework substantially shaped French intellectual culture and structuralism as broader 20th-century movement (separately enriched as broader Saussurean Structuralism in linguistics batch). Lévi-Strauss's work substantially declined in influence from the 1970s onward as post-structuralism (Derrida, Foucault, others), interpretive anthropology (Geertz), and practice theory (Bourdieu) provided substantively different framings. Substantial subsequent critique includes Adam Kuper's The Reinvention of Primitive Society (2005) and broader empirical concerns about Lévi-Strauss's analyses of specific kinship systems and myths.

Core components

  • Cultural phenomena as systems of differences
  • Binary oppositions (raw/cooked, nature/culture, male/female, etc.)
  • Universal cognitive structures producing cross-cultural commonalities
  • Mythemes as basic units of mythological analysis
  • Substantial four-volume Mythologiques (1964-1971)
  • Connection to Saussurean structural linguistics
  • Application to kinship, myth, classification, ritual
  • Distinction from Boasian Cultural Relativism (universalist rather than particularist)
  • Substantial 20th-century French intellectual influence
  • Decline in influence from 1970s onward

Primary use case

Major framework in 20th-century anthropology particularly French tradition; basis for substantial structural analyses of kinship, myth, classification systems; reference framework in anthropology education particularly outside North America; foundation for substantial structuralist tradition across humanities and social sciences; integration with broader structuralist intellectual movement; pedagogical foundation in anthropology curricula; influence on literary theory, cultural studies, philosophy; foundation for substantial subsequent (and post-) structuralist work.

Common criticisms

  • Substantial subsequent critique: Adam Kuper (The Reinvention of Primitive Society 2005, earlier work) substantially documented empirical concerns about Lévi-Strauss's analyses of specific kinship systems — many of his foundational examples involve substantial misreading or selective interpretation of ethnographic sources
  • post-structuralist critique (Derrida, Foucault) substantially challenged structuralist confidence about systematic underlying meaning
  • the framework's emphasis on universal cognitive structures has been argued to substantially overstate cross-cultural commonalities while underweighting substantive cultural particularity
  • binary opposition analysis often produces oppositions that reflect analyst's commitments rather than substantive cultural reality — critics including Mary Douglas argued binary thinking is itself culturally specific
  • practical anthropological fieldwork has substantially shifted away from structuralist methodology toward interpretive, practice-theoretical, and other framings
  • the framework's mid-20th-century French intellectual context shapes specific commitments that don't always travel
  • commercial popular invocation often presents structuralism in oversimplified form
  • integration with substantial empirical findings about cognition and culture has been complicated by subsequent cognitive science research
  • tendency for structural analyses to produce elegant patterns that may not substantively reflect how cultural participants understand their own practices
  • cross-cultural application of European-developed framework has substantially privileged European intellectual concerns
  • recent anthropology has substantially returned to ethnographic particularity rather than structural-universal claims.
